# WealthSim — Research and Design Map

This table separates what published research supports from the product decisions made in WealthSim's design. None of the sources below validates WealthSim's custom scores, personas, thresholds, or translations.

| Source | Supported claim | Adaptation in WealthSim | Assumptions made | Validation still needed |
|--------|-----------------|------------------------|-----------------|------------------------|
| Holt & Laury (2002), *Risk Aversion and Incentive Effects*, AER. [doi](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/000282802762024700) | Paired lottery choices with explicit probabilities and outcomes can reveal risk preferences. Incentive strength affects measured risk aversion. | Ch1 presents three paired contracts with explicit outcome probabilities (0.3/0.5/0.7 chance of the high outcome). Neither option dominates in any pair. | The game uses fictional city credits, not real monetary payoffs; hypothetical incentives may not produce the same behaviour as real ones. Three pairs cannot fit a precise risk-aversion coefficient. | Whether paired-contract choices in a fictional city setting correlate with the choices Holt & Laury measure. |
| Frederick, Loewenstein & O'Donoghue (2002), *Time Discounting and Time Preference: A Critical Review*, AER. [doi](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/002205102320161311) | Intertemporal choices reflect more than pure time preference; urgent needs and uncertainty about future rewards confound measurement. | Ch4 offers a near-term (festival) and a deferred (university) benefit. A third option (repair) is explicitly tagged as a constraint, not a preference, and excluded from the timing pattern. | The two-option comparison is far simpler than the matched reward structures needed to isolate discount rates. The repair option is a product shortcut, not a preference-reversal test. | Whether the festival/university choice isolates timing preference from other factors (cost, uncertainty, social pressure). Matched reward structures at different dates would be needed for a discount-rate estimate. |
| Odean (1998), *Are Investors Reluctant to Realize Their Losses?*, JF. [doi](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/0022-1082.00072) | Brokerage-account evidence of greater willingness to realise gains than losses; the paper examines alternative explanations carefully. | Ch9 presents matched projects whose only difference is purchase-price history (forward prospects are identical). A fourth trial explicitly varies forward prospects to distinguish disposition-effect sensitivity from rational selling. | The game's three matched trials are far fewer than the brokerage data examined by Odean. The scenarioId `ch9:matched_gain_loss` prevents conflation with the prospects trial. | Whether the observed sell_winner/sell_loser ratio in three trials replicates across players; whether it predicts real-world behaviour. |
| Moore et al. (2017), *Confidence Calibration in a Multiyear Geopolitical Forecasting Competition*, Management Science. [doi](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2525) | Studies confidence relative to forecasting accuracy across many repeated forecasts by the same person. | Ch10 reports a Brier score over four binary forecasts collected at chapter gates. The result explicitly labels this "forecast accuracy" not "calibration" and states that four forecasts are insufficient to describe a stable tendency. | Geopolitical forecasting findings do not transfer directly to a fictional city economy. Four forecasts cannot establish overconfidence; they are reported descriptively only. | Stable overconfidence would require many more forecasts over several sessions. Convergent validity with established calibration measures is unexamined. |
| Morewedge et al. (2015), *Debiasing Decisions*, Policy Insights from the Behavioral and Brain Sciences. [doi](https://doi.org/10.1177/2372732215600886) | Two experiments found persistent reductions in specific biases trained; games with personalised feedback and practice outperformed passive video. | After the result screen, one equivalent practice decision is offered, tagged `phase:'practice'` and excluded from the session profile. The result says explicitly that one attempt cannot show durable change. | The biases Morewedge et al. studied were not identical to WealthSim's targets. Effect sizes from that paper are not claimed for WealthSim. | Whether a single post-feedback practice trial transfers to new decisions; whether any transfer persists over days or weeks. |
| Lusardi & Mitchell (2014), *The Economic Importance of Financial Literacy*, JEL. [doi](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jel.52.1.5) | Reviews the relationship between financial knowledge and economic outcomes. Knowledge and behaviour are related but distinct. | WealthSim keeps knowledge (GRV / bAV / private provision) separate from the behavioural profile. Failure to understand an option is not scored as a personality trait. | The game does not measure financial literacy formally. Retirement education content is general and has not been verified by a pension specialist. | Whether the retirement education content (GRV / bAV / Säule 3) is accurate and up to date. Whether separating knowledge from behaviour is achieved in practice. |

## Design choices without a research basis

The following are product decisions, not parameters established by any of the sources above:

- Number of chapters (10), trials per chapter (3 in Ch9), forecasts (4)
- Coverage thresholds: 3 observations for `repeated`, 2 for `limited`
- The specific districts (Housing, Transport, Technology, Energy) and their economic parameters
- The six cube allocation mechanic and HHI concentration reporting
- Persona names and descriptions (if retained)
- The 15–20 minute target duration
- The exact phrasing of result templates in English and German
